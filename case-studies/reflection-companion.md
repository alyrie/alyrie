# Reflection Companion

**Private structured reflection and knowledge system**

## The problem

A useful reflection tool needs to be easier than maintaining a journal system, but more durable than having isolated conversations with an AI assistant.

The goal was to create a deliberately simple daily experience while building enough structure underneath it for entries to become searchable, organized, and useful over time.

## What I built

I designed a private application around daily structured input, persistent records, searchability, tagging, and an interface tailored to one person's actual habits rather than a generic productivity workflow.

My work included:

- Product definition
- UX and visual direction
- Authentication and access planning
- Data-model design
- Persistent daily-log storage
- Search and organization strategy
- Deployment
- Cost-conscious infrastructure decisions

## Design decisions

### Simplicity at the surface
The user should be able to open the application, record what matters, and leave. The complexity belongs in the system, not in the daily interaction.

### Structured enough to become useful
Entries are treated as durable records rather than disposable chat history, making future search, tagging, retrieval, and analysis possible.

### Designed for a real user
The interface and workflow were built around a specific person's preferences and behavior. That constraint improved the product because it forced every feature to justify the effort it asks of the user.

### Privacy matters
Personal reflection can contain highly sensitive information. Authentication, persistence, and access boundaries were therefore core architectural concerns.
